1. The Convoy Effect

In FCFS, P1 runs for 10 time units before the other 
processes can start. This makes P2 wait 10 units and P3 
wait 12 units, even though both are short tasks. This is 
the convoy effect. With Round Robin and a quantum of 3, 
P2 and P3 get CPU time earlier and finish sooner.

2. Choosing the Time Quantum

A very large quantum, such as 100, lets each process 
in this experiment finish in one turn, so Round Robin 
behaves like FCFS. A very small quantum causes frequent 
context switches in a real OS. This overhead can reduce 
efficiency, although the simulator does not measure it.

3. AI Inference Scheduling

Round Robin lets requests take turns generating tokens. 
A short request can finish without waiting for a long 
response to complete. This can reduce the wait for the 
first token and make an AI service feel more responsive. 
In the simulation, User B finishes before User A, but the 
script does not measure the actual Time To First Token.
